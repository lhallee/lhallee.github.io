Research

A model learns whatever separates its labels.

Sometimes that is biology, and often it is a shortcut that looks exactly like biology until somebody changes the test. Do I think every high score in the field is a mirage? No, but the shortcuts are common enough that I keep three threads of work running, which are catching the shortcut, building better representations, and designing proteins that have to survive a real assay.

01 / Evaluation

Did the model learn the biology?

High scores are cheap when the data leaks, so these papers find the leak, name it, and report what is left once it is closed.

embeddings, colored by species

BMC Bioinformatics, 2026

Protein language models are accidental taxonomists

In multi-species interaction datasets the true pairs usually share a species while randomly sampled negatives do not, and protein language model embeddings can tell whether two proteins share a taxonomic origin, so a model can ace the benchmark by learning phylogeny instead of interaction. When we restricted the negatives to pairs from the same species the shortcut closed and performance dropped, although those models still beat single-species ones, which means multi-species data helps once it is curated with care.

Paper

bioRxiv, 2026

Protify, and what a benchmark ranking is worth

Protify benchmarks 35 pretrained models on 61 datasets from one command, and running it at scale was humbling for anyone who likes a leaderboard. Across 32 supervised tasks no single model dominated, because 14 different models took a top score and the margins fell inside run-to-run variability. On enzyme optimal pH, probes on pooled embeddings looked strong until the split controlled for homology, where every one of them fell to an R2 near zero, so a ranking says as much about the evaluation design as it does about the models.

02 / Representation

What should a protein model know, and what should it cost?

New vocabularies and new attention, for models that know more about a protein than its sequence statistics.

one head, two triangles

bioRxiv, 2024

Annotation Vocabulary (might be) all you need

The Annotation Vocabulary is a transformer-readable language of protein properties built from structured ontologies. A representation model trained with it for $3 of commercial compute produced state-of-the-art embeddings on 5 of 15 common datasets, and a generative model prompted with annotations alone wrote sequences that return significant BLAST hits whose enrichment matches the prompt, even when the target has low identity to the entire training set.

PaperCode

arXiv, 2026

Dual Triangle Attention

Bidirectional attention is blind to position without positional embeddings, while causal attention is not, because its triangular mask carries order for free. Dual Triangle Attention splits each head into a past-and-self triangle and a future-and-self triangle, which keeps that positional signal in both directions with no added parameters. It learns position without positional embeddings on a synthetic probe, and with rotary embeddings it gave the best context extension in masked language modeling on both natural language and proteins.

Paper

Scientific Reports, 2025

Mixture of experts for biological text

Sentence similarity models struggle to compress a research paper into one useful vector, so we trained BERT models to predict whether two abstracts are cited together in a third paper across several biomedical domains. Every MLP block is copied into distinct experts that are routed by special tokens, which keeps the throughput exactly the same as a regular transformer.

03 / Design

From representation to a protein that binds.

One model that both reads and writes sequences, and designs that were measured at the bench.

masked diffusion on HSP90

arXiv, 2025

Diffusion Sequence Models

A Diffusion Sequence Model is a protein language model trained with masked diffusion, so one set of weights both embeds and generates. It writes biomimetic sequences from inputs with 90% of the tokens corrupted, its representations match or exceed those of similarly sized models, and a variant conditioned on a target sequence generates binders.

Bench result

EGFR binders tighter than the cetuximab control

In third-party biolayer interferometry, one designed EGFR binder reached a dissociation constant of 630 pM against 3.33 nM for the cetuximab scFv control, and six designs bound more tightly than that control. The measurements are public, and the drawing shows the receptor held by cetuximab, the antibody behind that control.

SpeedrunningPLMs

How good a protein masked language model can one GPU train against a fixed clock? The data, objective, clock, evaluator, and model shape never change, so the only thing left to improve is the recipe, and a contribution is a new module or a recorded run. The 1 hour record sits within 0.03 nats of ESM2 8M, which trained for days and scores 2.6253 on the same residues.

Company

Synthyra

Whatever a pathogen does to a cell, it starts by binding, and binding can be predicted.

Synthyra is the Public Benefit LLC I cofounded in 2024. We are in talks with government, industry, and research organizations about three uses of our models, which are understanding how pathogens work, finding what treats them, and keeping new ones from being made through DNA and protein synthesis services.

Visit synthyra.comSee the public demos

Those sound like three problems, and they are one. A virus gets into a cell because one of its proteins fits one of ours, a drug stops it by binding a protein the infection depends on, and a dangerous sequence is dangerous because of what its protein will bind once somebody makes it. Each comes down to what binds what, and that is the question Atlas, our model, answers for any protein from its sequence alone.

Understand

How a pathogen works ↗

Infection starts when a pathogen's proteins bind the host's. Atlas scores every pathogen protein against every host protein and ranks the contacts a laboratory should test first, so a response team has a short list on day one and the bench confirms it.

 Treat

What stops it ↗

For a drug the question runs the other way, and Atlas predicts which proteins a small molecule binds, including the ones it should leave alone. In the public demo it scores three Andes hantavirus proteins against every drug the FDA has approved and shows the structures behind its top two candidates. Both are predictions for a laboratory to test, and both are covered by filed provisional patent applications that name Jason Gleghorn and me as the inventors.

 Prevent

What should not be made ↗

DNA synthesis screening compares an order against lists of known dangerous sequences, so a sequence that is new or redesigned matches nothing and passes without a second look. Atlas reads what a DNA or protein order is and what it would do, and the orders that need a person reach one along with the reason.

About

Biochemistry as a programmable medium.

I am motivated by safe computational systems that help us understand the universe at every level of abstraction, and most of my effort goes toward high-fidelity modeling of the protein universe, which is the work that turns biochemistry into a programmable medium.

I believe real biochemical mastery can unlock organic carbon capture, better crops, efficient circular economies, and major advances in medicine.
